# City Chronicle 3 (Летопись города 3) — Game Design Document

**Lens: Systems Economist.** One coin on the surface, a real ledger underneath. Every number the player sees has a visible cause. The economic formulas (money, goods, population, happiness, land value, traffic, trade, land) run in aggregate form in the pacing simulation `sim-economist.mjs` (same folder). The roster, tech table, budget and pacing tables below were generated by that script, not typed by hand. Per-tile spatial effects, events, the offline chest and visuals are specified here but are not part of the simulation.

Design pillars:
1. **One coin, deep ledger.** The HUD shows only money (деньги), in the face of the current era. Seven goods, energy and knowledge sit one tap away in the Storage, City and Research panels.
2. **Cause → effect.** Each building card shows its ledger line: what it costs per year, what it earns or saves, and why ("Market: 3 500 customers × 7 = 24 500/yr; you have 2 900 residents → 83%").
3. **The calendar is a stopwatch.** One year is 25 s of game time, always. Eras open by achievement. The historical era years are the benchmark you race against: "Опередить историю".
4. **Land is the scarce factor.** Everyone plays the same 100 × 40 map. The deposits, beach and sea edge are identical for all, so land choices are strategy, not luck.

## 1. Vision and core loops

**The fantasy.** You run a city's treasury across 2 100 years. You build homes, the jobs to fill them and the services that keep people happy, and you trade your surplus for money. Then you watch the year counter: Middle Ages in the year 39, steam in the year 244, the metro in the year 641 — a whole millennium ahead of real history.

**30-second loop: the fiscal year.** The year ring on the HUD fills every 25 s. When it closes, a small toast shows the year's result ("1347: +12 400 · tax 3 100 · trade 2 200 · upkeep −4 900"). This replaces the Clash of Clans "tap to collect" with something automatic but satisfying. Between closes the player taps buildings, reads their ledger cards, places one or upgrades one (crews permitting), and adjusts the tax slider.

**5-minute loop (about 12 years): find the bottleneck.** The Treasury (Казна) panel ranks the city's top three constraints by money lost per year, for example "Unemployment 14% → −2 300/yr", "Water coverage 82% → −1 100/yr" or "Congestion 1.3 on the north avenue → −900/yr". The player fixes one. Over the same window a tech completes and a Chronicle task finishes.

**Session loop (30–60 min): one structural step.** Enter an era, buy a land strip, lay a rail line to the mountain portal, or retune trade (sell thresholds, port upgrade).

**Long-term loop (≈6 h of 1× play for a typical player): race history.** The arc runs through seven eras, a 14-strip land race to both map edges, a rising population cap and the Chronicle lead. After 2100 a sandbox follows: fill the map, hold World Expos (Всемирная выставка), climb the leaderboard.

**Why it is fun (the economist's argument).**
- *Trade-offs, not chores.* Each money source has diminishing returns: exports saturate a market, commerce saturates customers, tourism saturates visitor demand, taxes are capped by happiness. So the best next move changes all the time and is never "spam X".
- *Readable optimisation.* The tax optimum is a rule of thumb the player can learn: every 1.5 happiness points above 60 buys 1% of tax for free (§6). Roads have a capacity number, and so does each metro station.
- *The race.* Speed buttons do not help the Chronicle lead, because the lead is measured in game years. Only good economics does. Everyone on the shared map races the same benchmark.

**What I took and why.**

| From | Taken | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Classic SimCity | Budget (taxes vs upkeep), tax elasticity through happiness, land value, traffic capacity and transit, service coverage, a jobs↔housing balance (the RCI idea reduced to "workers vs jobs") | These systems create real interlocking decisions. |
| SimCity BuildIt | One soft currency; material chains where missing materials can always be bought for money; expansions bought as land strips; population driven by service coverage; road upgrades | Simple surface, deep chain, and no dead-ends. |
| Clash of Clans | Town Hall gates eras (the checklist), crews/builders as a parallel-construction limit, a compact HUD, the offline chest ritual | Clear progression, plus a mobile-friendly rhythm. |
| Not taken | Premium currency, monetised timers, automatic zoning | Single player; v3 keeps placement explicit as in v2. |

## 2. Time

**Calendar.** `year = startYear + gameTime / secondsPerYear`, with `startYear = 0` and `secondsPerYear = 25`. One sim tick is 1 game second, and every flow in content is defined per year (/25 per tick). The year is derived and never stored separately, so it can never jump. Era changes do not touch it. The server keeps a denormalised year only for the leaderboard.

**Fiscal year = calendar year.** Every rate in the UI is "per year" (/год): income, upkeep, production and trade. The yearly close is the budget statement.

**What the HUD shows.** `1347 г.`, the era name, a ring that fills over the 25 s of the current year, a season glyph, and the speed buttons. Months are not shown (12 months in 25 s would just flicker). Tapping the year opens the Chronicle (lead, history benchmarks, tasks).

**Speeds.** Pause, ×1, ×2, ×5 (testers also ×20, ×50). Speed multiplies game time, so the economy and the calendar speed up together. The Chronicle lead is therefore identical at any speed: speed is pure convenience, and the leaderboard stays fair.

**Seasons and day/night: a scene clock.** A 25-second year cannot carry visual seasons: there would be a 6-second winter, and a strobe at ×5. So the scene runs its own clock:
- `sceneTime += realDt × min(speed, 2)` (so ×5 and the tester speeds never strobe);
- one day/night cycle = 360 s of scene time (6 real minutes at ×1, 3 minutes at ×2 or faster);
- one season = 3 days = 18 min, so the visual year lasts 72 min;
- seasons are **cosmetic** (snow, foliage, light, weather particles) and only change *event weights* (fires in summer, flu in winter). There are no seasonal production modifiers, so the economic maths stays readable;
- the framing shown to players: "the year counter is the chronicle; the scene shows one living day of it" (as in Civilization's turn-years).

**Offline: the chronicle pauses.** While the player is away, game time does not advance, so the year never jumps on reload and nobody loses Chronicle lead by sleeping. This also removes v2's 3 600-tick catch-up on load (a mobile freeze). In its place is the **Treasury Chest** (Сундук казны) on the town hall:
- it fills linearly over 8 real hours, up to `4 × average net income per year over the last 10 years`;
- the player taps it to collect;
- impact on the race: at most about 12 game-years of income per day, which is under 2% of a 6-hour game, so it is a ritual and not an exploit;
- server check on every sync: `Δ gameTime ≤ Δ wallTime × maxSpeed` (5, or 50 for testers), and chest claims are recorded server-side.

### 3.1 Requirements to enter each era (Town Hall checklist)

| Enter | Population | Town hall | Gross revenue last year | Techs | Key buildings | Fee |
|---|---:|---|---:|---|---|---:|
| e1000 | 200 | L2 Дом вождя | 900 | agriculture, writing, mathematics | school, market | 4 000 |
| e1500 | 600 | L3 Ратуша | 2 500 | metallurgy, smithing, brickmaking | 2 smithies, kiln, temple L2 | 20 000 |
| e1800 | 1 600 | L4 Дворец | 9 000 | printing, navigation, banking | port, glassworks, architect bureau | 80 000 |
| e1900 | 3 600 | L5 Городская управа | 30 000 | steam_engine, steelmaking, railways | rail station, steel mill, coal mine | 250 000 |
| e2000 | 7 000 | L6 Мэрия | 80 000 | electricity, reinforced_concrete, subway | power plant, cement plant, 2 metro stations | 700 000 |
| e2100 | 13 000 | L7 Сити-холл | 180 000 | computers, semiconductors, jet_age | electronics fab, airport L2, skyscraper | 1 600 000 |

**Why these.** Each checklist has five parts, and each part names a different economic muscle:
- **Size** (population): the labour and consumer base.
- **Fiscal capacity** (gross revenue): this cannot be faked with one token building; it needs a balanced economy.
- **The era's defining chain or network:** metal and tools → glass, port and architects → steam, steel and rail → power, concrete and metro → chips, jets and offices. Each key building is also the one the next era's economy leans on.
- **Three signature techs,** which pace knowledge production.
- **The fee:** about 3–6 years of the threshold gross. It works as the era's capital project and as a money sink.

The sim shows that the binding constraint changes from era to era: population and fee in Antiquity, fee and town hall in the middle eras, and key buildings plus land value in e2000 (the airport L2 and the skyscraper's LV 60 need planning).

### 3.2 What an era unlocks
- New building levels whose `era` matches (§10), new road classes (§8) and the era's techs (§11).
- The era's Chronicle tasks and event pool (§13).
- The era's money face (§4).
- Land: the next expansion ring becomes purchasable (§7). Land is never *granted*.

### 3.3 Gradual transition: what changes, when

| Changes at once (on entry) | Changes over time | Never changes |
|---|---|---|
| Unlock lists (levels, techs, roads, tasks, events); the next land ring can be bought | Sky and terrain palette blend over 20 game-years (500 s) | Calendar rate and the year itself |
| The HUD money face, with a one-off "Денежная реформа" toast; the amount is unchanged | HUD accent colour blends over 20 years | Existing buildings and roads keep their level and look (a level is drawn in its own era's palette, as in v2) |
| The novelty bonus resets to +40% (§3.4) | New-era residents spawn at a share of `min(1, yearsInEra / 40)` as NPCs respawn; nobody is re-dressed at once | Prices of goods, wages of existing jobs, tax rate |
| Era fee is paid | Vehicle mix follows road class and era share (carts on cobble, cars only on asphalt and above), so it shifts as roads are upgraded | Land owned, deposits, map |
| | Ambient soundscape cross-fades over 10 years | Storage contents and money |

Wages are tied to the **workplace level's era**, and goods demand to the **home level's era**, not to the city's era. Income therefore rises as the player upgrades, not when the era flips. This is the economic core of "an era transition must not change everything".

### 3.4 "Ahead of history" (Опередить историю): the mechanic

Let `entryYear_k` be the year the city entered era k, `hist_k` the historical year, and `span_k = hist_{k+1} − hist_k` (100 for e2100).

1. **Lead (Опережение)** is shown in the Chronicle and on the leaderboard: `lead_k = hist_k − entryYear_k` in years (negative means behind). The live chip reads "Опережение 1 247 лет".
2. **Chronicle points** (score): `Σ_k k × max(0, lead_k)`. Later eras weigh more because historical eras get shorter (500 → 300 → 100 years), so a lead there is harder to keep. Par gets ≈28 500, slow ≈23 300, optimised ≈30 900 (sim).
3. **Novelty bonus** (Новизна), applied to exports and tourism: `novelty = 1 + clamp(0.1 × log2(span_k / max(0.5, yearsInEra)), 0, 0.4)`. On entering an era your goods and sights are "ahead of their time" (+40%). The bonus fades as you linger: in a 100-year era it is +40% for the first 6 years, +20% at 25 years and 0 at 100. The tooltip states the cause: "Модерн: you've been here 18 years; history needed 100 → +25%". This rewards advancing promptly in every era, not only the early ones.
4. **Diffusion** (Заимствование), the catch-up for players behind history: once `year > hist_{k+1}`, the techs of era k+1 and its fee are discounted by `d = clamp((year − hist_{k+1}) / 400, 0, 0.5)`. The tooltip: "The world already lives in the Renaissance: −23%." This never triggers for normal play (the slow profile still enters e2100 at year 1168), so it is a safety net for idle cities.

Shape of the race (par, sim): the lead grows from 961 to 1 556 years up to the Industrial era, then **shrinks** (1 458 → 1 358 → 1 247). History's last three eras are only 100 years apart, while a player needs 80–90 game-years each. "History is catching up" is the late-game tension.

### 5.4 Prices, trade capacity and storage

- **Base prices are fixed** (table 5.1). Prices are not era-indexed, so nothing jumps at an era change.
- **Import price:** `p × (1 + M)`, with `M = max(0.15, 0.60 − portMarkup − railMarkup)`. Port L1–L3 gives 0.20/0.25/0.30 and rail L1–L3 gives 0.15/0.20/0.25 (the best instance of each type counts).
- **Export price:** `p × (1 − D) × novelty / (1 + S_r / depth_r)`, with `D = max(0.10, 0.50 − portDisc − railDisc − (goods: airportDisc))`.
  - Port gives 0.15/0.20/0.25, rail 0.10/0.15/0.20, airport (goods only) 0.10/0.15/0.20.
  - **Saturation** `S_r` is the recent export volume. Each sale adds to it, and it decays with a 1-year time constant: `S ← S × e^(−dt/25)`.
  - **Market depth:** `depth_r = base_r × eraDepth × (1 + 0.5·[port] + 0.5·[rail] + 1.0·[airport, goods only])`, with `eraDepth = [1, 2, 4, 8, 14, 22, 32]`.
  - The UI shows a saturation bar: "Market 60% saturated → price −38%, recovers in ~1 year".
- **Trade capacity** `K` (units per year): town hall caravans (60 → 1 000 by TH level), plus markets (40–500), port (600/2 000/5 000; +25% in the lagoon strip E3), rail station (800/2 000/4 000), logistics warehouses (500/800), plus airport capacity for goods only (300/1 000/2 500), times (1 + logistics tech 20%). All automatic imports and exports draw on K as a rolling budget.
- **Storage:** one cap shared by each good: `300 + Σ storehouse + town hall storage`. Output above the cap is exported (if K allows) or wasted, and the card shows "wasted 120/yr".

### 5.5 Production chains

```
food      ← farm (land) · fishery (sea edge) · vertical farm
wood      ← forestry plantation (Лесхоз, land)
stone     ← quarry (touches mountain)
metal     ← mine (touches mountain on an ore vein)
fuel      ← coal mine (mountain, coal seam) · oil well (sea edge, oil field)
materials ← kiln (stone+wood → stone+fuel → stone) · glassworks (sand; wood → fuel) ·
            steel mill (metal+fuel → metal) · cement plant (stone+fuel → stone) · composites plant (metal+goods)
goods     ← smithy (metal+wood → metal+fuel → metal) · chemical plant (fuel → food) · electronics fab (metal+materials)
energy    ← windmill L2–3 · power plant (fuel) · solar farm
knowledge ← town hall · school · temple · architect bureau · district hub · data center
```
Several producers make the same good from different inputs. The cheapest route depends on *your* deposits and strips. For example, glassworks on sand need no stone, so they suit a city with few quarries.

### 5.6 Demand

| Good | Demand |
|---|---|
| food | Residents: `1 × P` per year |
| goods | Residents: `P × (Σ housing × gd)/Σ housing`, by home level |
| materials, metal, wood, stone | Construction bills, plus processor inputs |
| fuel | Power plants, mines L3, steel, cement, kilns L2, farms L4–5, quarry L4, water pump |
| energy | Homes from house L8/block L2, plus industry and services at e1900+ levels |

A deficit is auto-imported if money > 0 and trade capacity allows. Otherwise the shortfall bites: food −25 happiness × shortRatio, goods −15 × shortRatio, energy reduces efficiency and costs −20 happiness for powered homes.

### 5.7 Proof sketch: money neither explodes nor dead-ends

**No explosion: every source is bounded.**
- Exports: revenue for one good is `q × P_e / (1 + q/depth)` with `q` = units per year. That rises with `q` but never exceeds `P_e × depth`. At e2100 with port + rail + airport and the minimum discount, the asymptotes are: food 34.6k, wood 34.6k, stone 46.1k, metal 69.1k, materials 103.7k, goods 155.5k, fuel 92.2k. That is **≈536k per year in total** (×1.4 for a few years after era entry).
- Tourism: at most `13 × 300 × 32 ≈ 125k per year` (×1.4).
- Tax, domestic sales and commerce are proportional to population, and population is bounded by land: 4 000 land tiles. The sim sandbox reaches ~95–138k residents at ~20–30 gross per resident.

So gross income is bounded at about **5M per year**, and accumulated money grows at most linearly. Late sinks absorb it geometrically: the last strips (900k, 1.8M), TH L8 (3.24M), World Expos (`600k × 1.6ⁿ`, so the score bought with money grows only logarithmically).

**No dead-end.**
1. Upkeep can never push money below 0. Unpaid upkeep becomes **Arrears** (Задолженность): the unpaid share `u` halves coverage and production `× (1 − 0.5u)` until it is paid. The card shows "On strike: arrears 340/yr".
2. Construction, fees and strips can only be bought when affordable. Imports run only while money > 0.
3. Tax and domestic sales do not depend on upkeep, so income keeps flowing during arrears.
4. **Mothball** (Законсервировать) any building: zero upkeep, zero effect, reversible for free. The Advisor suggests the highest-upkeep building with spare coverage. Demolition refunds 50%.
5. **Loans** (Ссуда) at the town hall: up to 2 × last year's gross at 8% per year (banking: 3× at 5%), auto-repaid over 10 years. One at a time.
6. Minimum economy: the town hall L1 itself provides 6 housing, 4 jobs, caravans and storage, so a city can always restart.

In all three sim profiles the treasury never went below 0 (lowest values 18–1 500) and the arrears rule never fired (0 seconds in arrears). Treasury share of upkeep at era entry: 16–39% of gross.

## 6. Population and city

All constants are in §15. Per-home values are computed on structural change, using spatial indexes. City-wide terms are computed per tick in O(1).

**Housing and occupancy.**
- `H = Σ housing(level) × (1 + techHousing)`, counting connected homes only.
- `occupancy(home) = clamp((hap_home − 25) / 35, 0, 1)`. Happiness 60 gives full homes, 42.5 gives half-full, 25 or below gives empty.
- `P* = Σ_homes housing × occupancy(home)`.
- Growth when `P < P*`: `dP/dt = (0.3 × (P* − P) + 1) / 25` per second (people take years to move in).
- Decline when `P > P*`: `dP/dt = −0.8 × (P − P*) / 25`.

**Workforce and jobs.**
- Workforce `W = 0.45 × P`.
- Jobs are filled in priority classes: **services/civic/transport/power → food → raw → industry → commerce**. Each class gets a staffing ratio `s_c`. A labour shortage first idles shops, never wells. (This fixes v2's "global ratio starves everyone".)
- Unemployment `u = 1 − filled/W`.
- Modern workplaces employ more people: job counts scale by level era `[1, 1, 1.3, 2.0, 2.8, 3.6, 4.5]` (already applied in the roster).

**Happiness at a home.**
```
hap = 35
    + Σ_services weight_s × covered_s × quality_s     water 12, health 10, education 8, culture 8, leisure 7
    + min(8, landmark/park aura)  + road class bonus (0..5)  + tech bonuses
    − 1.5 × (τ − 8)                       tax (τ in %, 8% is neutral)
    − 60 × max(0, u − 0.05)               unemployment above 5%
    − 25 × foodShort − 15 × goodsShort − 20 × (1 − energyRatio) (powered homes)
    − 0.7 × pollution_here − 0.5 × noise_here
    − 10 × clamp(ρ_here − 0.9, 0, 1.5)    congestion on the home's road
    + moods (events)
clamped to 0..100
```

**Services and coverage.** A home is *covered* by service s if it lies within Chebyshev radius R of an s-building. `quality_s = min(1, ΣCap_s / P_covered_s)`. Radius and capacity per level are listed in the roster. Example: the well L1 serves 50 residents within radius 3; the water treatment plant L5 serves 5 000 within radius 7. There are five services; health starts at e1000, so Antiquity's maximum happiness is lower.

**Tax elasticity (the visible rule).** Raising τ by 1% costs 1.5 happiness. Occupancy is flat (100%) above happiness 60. So the free tax headroom is `τ* ≈ 8 + (hap₀ − 60) / 1.5`, where hap₀ is happiness at τ = 8. For hap₀ = 70, τ* ≈ 14.7%. Beyond that, each extra 1% loses about 4.3% of residents, and with them domestic sales and commerce: the Laffer curve. The tax slider previews both "+tax" and "−residents".

**Land value (LV, 0..100) per tile.**
```
LV = 30
   + coast(y): land rows 30..39 → 1.2 × (y − 29) (up to +12); sand tiles +15
   + 2.5 × Σ services covering the tile (weighted by quality)
   + min(20, Σ aura of parks / manors / markets / bureau within radius)
   + road bonus of the best adjacent road class (0..12)
   + transit: metro station ≤4 tiles +10/12/14, rail station ≤6 tiles +5/6/8
   − pollution − noise (airport, avenue)
   − 10 × clamp(ρ − 0.8, 0, 1.5)
```
LV matters in two ways:
1. **Tax:** a resident's tax multiplier is `0.6 + 0.8 × LV/100` (0.6 at LV 0, 1.4 at LV 100).
2. **High-rise gates:** house L8 needs LV 40, L9 50, L10 60, L11 65; block L4 55, L5 60; skyscraper L1 60, L2 70. The card says "Needs LV 50 (here 43: add a park or a metro station)".

**Pollution.** `pollution(tile) = Σ_sources amount × (1 − d / (R + 1))` for `d ≤ R`. It lowers happiness and LV, which makes the mountain foot a natural industrial belt and the coast a residential belt.

**District policies** (kept from v2, re-stated economically; one per district hall):

| Policy | Effect |
|---|---|
| Historic | Each building gets +2 attraction per era of age |
| Industrial | +20% output, +30% pollution, −5 happiness |
| Residential | +15% housing, +8 happiness |
| Business | +25% commerce revenue |
| Green | −50% pollution, +10 happiness |

**Crews** (Бригады, CoC builders): 2/2/3/3/4/4/5/6 parallel constructions by TH level. Build times are v2's per-level buildTime in game seconds, reduced by techs (−10% to −15% each, floor 40%).

Transit built into roads: tram boulevards remove 15% of trips on their tiles, avenues (buses) 8%, smart avenues 12%. The player chooses **boulevard** (land value, happiness, tram) for residential streets and **avenue** (capacity) for arterials. Junctions, lane counts and decor are rendered from the class, as procedural road parts.

**Traffic model** (cheap, indexed, recomputed on structural change and once per fiscal year):
```
trips generated at a tile:
  homes:    residents × tripsPerResident[era]   (0.3, 0.4, 0.5, 0.7, 1.0, 1.4, 1.6: carts → cars)
  jobs:     filledJobs × 0.25
  visitors: 0.02 per visitor
local demand D(x,y)   = box-sum of trips over a 9×9 window   (summed-area table, O(W×H) = 6 000 cells)
local capacity C(x,y) = box-sum of road-tile capacity over the same window × 0.3
transit removal: each metro/rail station serves trips in its radius
                 share = min(0.6, stationCap / D_inRadius)
ρ(x,y) = D × (1 − tram/bus share − transit share) / C
```
Effects of congestion ρ on a tile:

| Affected | Effect |
|---|---|
| Workplaces | Productivity × `1 / (1 + 0.6 × max(0, ρ − 1))` (ρ = 2 → 62%) |
| Homes | Happiness `−10 × clamp(ρ − 0.9, 0, 1.5)` |
| LV | `−10 × clamp(ρ − 0.8, 0, 1.5)` |
| Vehicles | Density on screen ∝ ρ; speed ∝ class speed / max(1, ρ) |

The traffic overlay colours roads by ρ, and the card says "This block makes 1 240 trips/yr; roads here carry 900 → 138%: upgrade to Проспект or add a metro station". In the sim, par upgrades its roads in steps from the Modern era on; road mix at the end: 25% boulevard, 25% avenue, 50% smart.

**Why it is fair on a shared map.**
- Every component is measured in game time or bounded by the identical map: same deposits, same 4 000 land tiles, same strip prices.
- Speed buttons and session length cannot buy lead (§2). The offline chest is capped.
- In the long run population converges to the land cap (~140k). Ranking then falls to Chronicle points (pure economic speed), achievements and Expos (geometric cost, so logarithmic in money).

Anti-cheat (the save is client-authoritative, so the server checks plausibility):
- era entry game-times are stored server-side, and lead is computed there;
- `Δ gameTime ≤ Δ wall × maxSpeed`;
- `population ≤ housing capacity` (from content);
- `money` growth must be plausible against the level/structure ledger;
- the score is recomputed on the server from stored fields.

**What it simulates.** It ticks the real formulas of §§2–9 once per game second: priority labour, energy, production with input imports, residents' food and goods, exports with saturation, coverage, LV, happiness, occupancy, the four money streams, upkeep with arrears, research, crews, strips, road-mix upgrades, transit and era advance. It is non-spatial: coverage is capacity plus area, traffic uses the network-wide ρ, and land is footprint × (1 + 0.7 roads) × slack plus a pollution buffer. Not simulated: random events (expected value ≈0), the offline chest, the metro mobility bonus, level crossings and per-tile variation.

A greedy player values every possible build or upgrade as `Δannual value / cost` and picks the best affordable one. It saves up when an unaffordable option is ≥ k× better, keeps a net-income margin, mothballs buildings during arrears, buys strips when land or deposits run out, upgrades 25% of roads at a time when congested or when land value blocks high-rises, and saves for era fees (the optimised player also borrows them).

## 16. Risks, open questions, and what to cut first

**Risks.**
1. **The pacing rests on a heuristic AI.** Real players will vary more: they will under-build services and over-build exports. Mitigation: live-tune `costScaleByEra`, `ringCost` and the era fees from server telemetry (era entry times are already stored for the score).
2. **Late-era lead of 1 200+ years.** Arriving in the Near Future in the year 853 is an intended alternate-history joke, but some players may find it odd. Option: show the lead as a percentage as well.
3. **The economy is dense for mobile.** Mitigation: the panels are progressive, and the Advisor's "top-3 constraints" list carries most players.
4. **Per-tile land value, traffic and coverage** must stay indexed: summed-area tables for traffic, spatial buckets for auras and coverage, recomputed on structural change and yearly. Never per tick, never O(N²).
5. **Deposits on a shared map could create one dominant build order.** Mitigation: two viable sides (east metal, west fuel), several producers per good, and imports as an always-available fallback.
6. **Offline freeze vs idle expectations.** Some players expect their city to grow while away. The chest provides the ritual; continuous idle income would break the fair race.
7. **Content migration.** 148 levels need new cost/upkeep/jobs numbers. Mitigation: generate them with the sim's content table and the scale factors (as done here). The validators (TS + PHP) need fields `upkeep`, `lvReq`, `gd`, `transit`, `trade`, `markup`, `discount`, `access`, `cust`/`rev`, and `place` values `mountain:*`, `sea`, `sea:oil`, `sand`, `land|sand`. The building-size limit must allow 12×4 (airport) and 4×2.

**Open questions.**
- Should commerce depend on goods being in stock (shops need goods), or stay a pure population function as now (simpler)?
- Should the novelty bonus also apply to taxes (+10%) to make era entry feel bigger?
- Is a fixed 85% sell threshold enough UX, or do we need a per-good trade policy (keep / sell / sell all)?
- Should district policies stay one per district hall, or become city-wide laws?

**Cut first if scope must shrink** (in order):
1. Port L2–L3: keep L1 on the reused model; rail covers late trade.
2. Metro L2–L3 models: reuse the L1 entrance and just raise capacity.
3. Airport L3: L2 becomes the final level.
4. Events beyond the 6 core ones.
5. Loans and the diffusion catch-up (rarely triggered).
6. Per-tile traffic: fall back to per-district ρ (district = 9×9 block).
7. Chemical and composites plants: fold their output into the electronics fab and the steel mill.
8. Expo: replace it with a flat late-game money→score sink.

Never cut: one currency with era faces, the constant calendar with the Chronicle lead, strips with deposits, the road classes as cross-sections, upkeep with arrears, and saturating trade. Together they are the game.
